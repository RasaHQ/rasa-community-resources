# PCOS Nutrition Guidelines (grounding reference, not medical advice)

Summarized from published dietitian guidance for PCOS (polycystic ovary
syndrome), which commonly involves insulin resistance and low-grade
inflammation.

## Low-glycemic-load eating
- Favor whole grains, legumes, and non-starchy vegetables over refined
  carbohydrates and sugary drinks.
- Pair carbohydrates with protein or fat (e.g. fruit with nuts, rice with
  beans and vegetables) to blunt blood-sugar spikes.

## Anti-inflammatory pattern
- Emphasize fatty fish, olive oil, nuts, seeds, colorful vegetables, and
  berries.
- Minimize fried foods, refined sugar, and heavily processed snacks.

## Fiber and protein
- Aim for consistent fiber intake (vegetables, legumes, whole grains) to
  support satiety and gut health.
- Adequate protein at each meal supports satiety and steadier blood sugar.

## Overlap with type 2 diabetes guidance
- The same low-glycemic-load approach used here is very similar to ADA
  carb-counting guidance — worth flagging to someone managing both PCOS and
  type 2 diabetes.

## What this is not
- Not a weight-loss program and not a substitute for treatment of insulin
  resistance, androgen-related symptoms, or fertility concerns — those stay
  with the treating clinician.
