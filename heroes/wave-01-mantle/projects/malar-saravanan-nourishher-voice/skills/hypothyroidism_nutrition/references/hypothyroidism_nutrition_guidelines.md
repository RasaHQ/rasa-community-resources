# Hypothyroidism Nutrition Guidelines (grounding reference, not medical advice)

Summarized from published dietitian/endocrine-society dietary guidance for
hypothyroidism. This is general nutrition education, not a substitute for a
clinician's care plan.

## Iodine
- Iodine is required to make thyroid hormone; both deficiency and excess can
  worsen thyroid function.
- Good moderate sources: iodized salt, dairy, eggs, seafood, seaweed (in
  small amounts — seaweed can easily provide far more than needed).
- Do not recommend high-dose iodine supplements; food sources are the safer
  default for general guidance.

## Selenium
- Selenium supports thyroid hormone conversion.
- Good sources: Brazil nuts (very concentrated — one or two a day is
  typically plenty), fish, eggs, sunflower seeds.

## Goitrogens (moderation, not elimination)
- Raw cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cauliflower, cabbage, kale) and soy
  contain compounds that can interfere with iodine uptake in very large,
  regular raw quantities.
- Cooking substantially reduces this effect. Normal, varied intake of these
  foods is not a concern for most people — the point is moderation of large
  raw quantities, not avoidance.

## General pattern
- Regular meals, adequate protein, and enough fiber support energy and
  digestion, which can be affected by low thyroid function.
- Highly processed, very low-calorie, or crash-diet patterns are discouraged
  — they can stress an already-slowed metabolism.

## Medication-timing interaction (food/drug, not dosage)
- Levothyroxine absorption is reduced by calcium, iron supplements,
  high-fiber foods, and soy when taken close together.
- Standard guidance: take levothyroxine on an empty stomach and wait several
  hours before those foods/supplements. This assistant repeats that timing
  reminder verbatim — it does not discuss dosage or brand switching.
